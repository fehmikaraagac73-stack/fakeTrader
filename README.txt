================================================================================

                             P A P E R   T R A D E R

                    A stock market simulator with fake money.

================================================================================

Everything in this application is invented. The companies do not exist, the
prices are generated by a simulation running on your own machine, the news
headlines are fabricated, and the money is not real. Nothing here connects to
a real exchange, no order ever leaves your computer, and nothing in it is
financial advice.

It is a game about learning what buying and selling feels like, with $10,000
you cannot actually lose.


================================================================================
1. HOW TO RUN IT
================================================================================

Double-click index.html.

That is genuinely all. It opens in your default browser and starts immediately.

  Or, from a terminal in this folder:

      Windows   :  start index.html
      macOS     :  open index.html
      Linux     :  xdg-open index.html

There is nothing to install. No Node, no Python, no web server, no internet
connection. The whole application is three files that run directly from disk:

      index.html    the page
      styles.css    the appearance
      scripts.js    the market, the portfolio and every screen

Works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. On first launch you will see a short
welcome screen; press "Start with $10,000" to begin.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1  WHERE YOUR PROGRESS IS SAVED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Your run is stored inside the browser itself (in "local storage"), not in a
file you can see. A few consequences worth knowing:

  * Closing the tab or the browser does not lose anything. Reopen and you are
    exactly where you left off.

  * Each browser keeps its own separate run. Opening the app in Chrome and in
    Edge gives you two independent $10,000 games with two different markets.

  * Clearing your browser's site data or cookies will erase your run.

  * Private/Incognito windows usually forget everything when closed.

You can export your transaction history (CSV or JSON) and a full copy of your
save from the Settings menu at any time.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2  TRYING THE PHONE LAYOUT ON A COMPUTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The app was designed for a phone first. To see that layout on a desktop:

      1. Press F12 to open developer tools
      2. Press Ctrl + Shift + M to toggle the device toolbar
      3. Pick a phone from the dropdown

This is also the only way to properly try the touch features on a desktop --
dragging across the chart to inspect prices, and swiping sheets away.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.3  IF SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Press Ctrl + Shift + R (Cmd + Shift + R on a Mac). This forces a full reload
and bypasses the browser's cache, which is the usual cause of a screen that
looks stale or half-drawn after an update.


================================================================================
2. HOW THE STOCK MARKET WORKS
================================================================================

This section explains the simulation honestly, because knowing the rules makes
the game more interesting rather than less.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  THE COMPANIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There are 42 fictional companies across 10 sectors: Technology, Healthcare,
Financials, Energy, Consumer, Industrials, Communication, Utilities, Materials
and Real Estate.

Each one has three fixed traits that give it a personality, and these are the
key to the whole thing:

  DRIFT       Its long-run tendency. A slight upward pull for most companies,
              stronger for the growth names.

  VOLATILITY  How violently it moves day to day. Northgrid Utility (NGRD) has
              a daily volatility near 1.2%; Vyra Interactive (VYRA) is above
              6%. Over a week that difference is enormous.

  BETA        How strongly it reacts to the market as a whole. A beta of 1.6
              means it amplifies market moves by roughly 60%; a beta of 0.3
              means it largely ignores them.

You can see all three on any stock's detail page under "Statistics", and the
About paragraph at the bottom of that page translates them into plain English.

The practical consequence: a utility stock and a meme stock genuinely do not
behave alike here. That is not decoration -- it is the same set of numbers
driving both the chart and the risk you take on.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2  HOW A PRICE ACTUALLY MOVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every price is the sum of three forces, recalculated continuously:

  1. A MARKET-WIDE FACTOR
     One shared number moves the entire market each day. When it is negative,
     almost everything falls together. This is why you cannot escape a bad day
     simply by owning more stocks -- diversification softens the blow, it does
     not remove it. How hard any one stock is hit depends on its beta.

  2. ITS OWN RANDOM MOVEMENT
     On top of the market, each company wanders on its own, scaled by its
     volatility. This is the part that lets one stock rise on a day when the
     market falls.

  3. NEWS EVENTS
     Roughly 3.5% of days, a company gets a headline -- an earnings beat, a
     downgrade, a lost customer, a regulatory inquiry. A headline gaps the
     price by somewhere between 3% and 14%, up or down.

     News is not cosmetic. The headline and the price move are the same event.
     If you hold or watch a stock when news breaks, a banner appears at the top
     of the screen telling you what happened and what it did to the price.

The prices update every 2 seconds, and the newest candle on the chart visibly
grows as it fills in, exactly as a real one would.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3  MARKET HOURS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The indicator at the top right always shows the real session:

      Market open     weekdays  9:30am - 4:00pm     full volatility
      After hours     weekdays  4:00am - 9:30am,
                                4:00pm - 8:00pm     reduced movement
      Closed          overnight                     very quiet
      Weekend                                       very quiet

Note that trading is never actually blocked -- a game where nothing happens
for two days out of seven would be a poor one. Instead, the market keeps
ticking but moves far less outside regular hours. Weekends and nights genuinely
feel slow, and Monday morning genuinely feels busy.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4  WHY PRICES DO NOT SPIRAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Left completely unchecked, a random walk eventually sends some stock to $0.02
and another to $40,000. Several guardrails prevent that:

  * A gentle pull back toward each company's long-run trend, so a stock that
    has run far above or below where it "should" be drifts back over time.
  * A hard price floor -- no stock ever reaches zero.
  * A cap on how extreme any single random move can be.
  * Orders at wildly implausible prices are rejected rather than left resting
    forever.

The result is a market that can absolutely halve or double a stock over months,
but will not produce nonsense.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5  THE MARKET IS REPRODUCIBLE (AND KEEPS RUNNING WHILE YOU ARE AWAY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Your run has a seed number -- visible at the top of the Settings screen. The
entire two-year price history of all 42 companies is calculated from that one
number. The same seed always produces exactly the same market.

This has a pleasant side effect: the market does not need to be running for
time to pass. Close the app for a week, come back, and the charts show a full
week of movement that "happened" while you were gone. Any limit or stop orders
you left resting are checked against that period and filled if the price
crossed them. You will see a banner telling you what filled while you were out.

That applies to your own account as much as to the stock charts. The net worth
line on Home and Portfolio is not a recording -- nothing about it is written
down as you play. It is recalculated from your transaction history and the
price engine every time you look at it, which means it can show what your
portfolio was worth at any minute in the past, including every minute the app
was shut. Leave for four days and you come back to four days of your account
rising and falling, not a flat line with a single jump at the end. The chart
has its own timeframe buttons -- 1D, 1W, 1M, 3M, 1Y and ALL -- for choosing how
far back to look.

Resetting your account in Settings generates a brand new seed, and therefore a
completely different market.


================================================================================
3. HOW TO PLAY
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  THE FIVE TABS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  HOME        Net worth, your holdings, watchlist, biggest movers of the day,
              recent activity, market news, and the leaderboard.

  SEARCH      Browse all 42 companies. Filter by sector, or search by ticker,
              company name or sector.

  PORTFOLIO   The full picture: cash, buying power, holdings value, realized
              and unrealized profit, allocation breakdowns, open orders, your
              track record and badges.

  HISTORY     Every transaction you have ever made.

  NOTES       Your notes and price alerts across all stocks.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2  BUYING AND SELLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Tap any stock to open its detail page. The Buy and Sell buttons are pinned to
the bottom of the screen. Both open the same order ticket.

There are only two actions in this app: BUY and SELL. There is no separate
"trade" -- with no counterparty to trade against, trading is simply those two
things in sequence.

THREE ORDER TYPES:

  MARKET   Fills immediately at the current price. The straightforward one.

  LIMIT    "Only fill at this price or better."
             - A buy limit fills when the price falls TO or BELOW your price.
             - A sell limit fills when the price rises TO or ABOVE your price.
           It waits until the market reaches you. It may never fill.

  STOP     A safety net, usually used to cap a loss.
             - A sell stop fills once the price falls THROUGH your level.
             - A buy stop fills once the price climbs THROUGH your level.
           This is the "stop-loss" -- set one below a position and you will be
           sold out automatically if it drops that far, even if you are not
           looking at the screen.

Limit and stop orders sit in your Open Orders list until they fill or you
cancel them. They are checked continuously, including retroactively for any
time the app was closed.

BUYING IN SHARES OR IN DOLLARS:

For market orders you can switch between entering a number of shares and
entering a dollar amount. "Buy $500 of NVTX" will buy fractional shares --
4.2231 shares is perfectly valid.

BEFORE YOU CONFIRM, the ticket shows you the current price, the estimated
number of shares, the estimated cost, your buying power, and what would be
left afterwards. If you are selling, it also shows the profit or loss you
would realize by selling at that price.

TO CONFIRM: swipe the green (or red) bar from left to right. If you are on a
keyboard, focus it with Tab and press Enter instead.

Orders that cannot work are refused with a plain explanation -- not enough
buying power, not enough shares, a stop-loss placed above the current price,
and so on. The error appears as you type rather than after you submit.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3  READING THE CHARTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  TIMEFRAMES     1D, 1W, 1M, 3M, 1Y and ALL, along the bottom of the chart.
                 1D and 1W are built from minute-by-minute data; longer ranges
                 use daily candles.

  LINE / CANDLES Toggle beneath the timeframes. Line is the calm default;
                 candles show the open, high, low and close of every interval.
                 A candle is green when it closed above where it opened, and
                 red when it closed below.

  THE COLOUR     The chart is green or red based on performance across the
                 WHOLE selected range, not the last flicker. A stock can tick
                 up while its 1Y chart stays red.

  DOTTED LINE    The price at the start of the selected range. Above it you
                 are up over that period; below it you are down.

  VOLUME BARS    The small bars beneath the chart show how much was traded in
                 each interval. Tall bars mean unusual activity -- often news.

  SCRUBBING      Press and drag along the chart. A crosshair follows your
                 finger and the big price at the top changes to the price at
                 that moment. Let go to snap back to live.

Dragging vertically still scrolls the page as normal -- only horizontal drags
control the crosshair.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4  UNDERSTANDING YOUR NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  CASH              Money not currently invested.

  BUYING POWER      Cash minus anything reserved by resting buy orders. This
                    is what you can actually spend right now. Cash and buying
                    power differ whenever you have a limit buy waiting.

  HOLDINGS VALUE    What your shares are worth at this moment.

  NET WORTH         Cash + holdings value. The number that actually tells you
                    whether you are winning.

  AVERAGE COST      What you paid per share on average. Buying the same stock
                    twice at different prices blends the two.

  UNREALIZED P/L    Profit or loss on paper, on shares you still hold. It
                    moves every 2 seconds and it is not yours until you sell.

  REALIZED P/L      Profit or loss actually locked in by selling. Calculated
                    against your average cost.

  TOTAL RETURN      Net worth versus the $10,000 you started with, in dollars
                    and as a percentage.

The net worth chart on Home and Portfolio is a record of your actual account
over time -- not any single stock. It is the honest scoreboard.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.5  TRANSACTION HISTORY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every buy and sell is recorded permanently with the date, time, ticker, side,
quantity, fill price, total value, and the cash balance it left behind.

  * Grouped by day, newest first, with the date pinned as you scroll.
  * Filter by side (buys / sells), by time range, or search by ticker.
  * A separate filter shows canceled and rejected orders, so your mistakes
    stay visible instead of quietly disappearing.
  * Tap any row for full detail, including the profit or loss realized on a
    sell and how the price has moved since that fill.
  * Export the whole record as CSV or JSON.

The history is never edited or deleted. It is the audit trail of your run.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.6  NOTES AND PRICE ALERTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the feature that turns intention into a reminder.

Write a note on any stock ("add more under $95 -- waiting on earnings") and
tag it as a Buy idea, a Sell target, or Research. Add it from the stock's
detail page, or from the "+ Note" button on the Notes tab.

Optionally attach a TARGET PRICE and a direction. The note then becomes a live
alert:

      "Alert me if NVTX falls to or below $95.00"

When the market reaches it, a banner appears at the top of the screen, a badge
appears on the Notes tab, and the note is pinned to the top of the Notes list
so you see it the moment you look. Alerts fire once; the bell icon on a
triggered note re-arms it.

Until it fires, each note shows how far the current price is from your target.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.7  SETTINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  REALISTIC FILLS   Off by default. Turn it on to add a bid/ask spread and
                    slippage to market orders -- you will buy slightly above
                    and sell slightly below the quoted price, exactly as in a
                    real market. It makes rapid in-and-out trading quietly
                    expensive, which is the lesson.

  COLOUR PALETTE    Switch green/red to blue/orange, which stays readable with
                    red-green colour blindness.

  REDUCE MOTION     Turns off transitions and the price flash animations.

  HAPTICS           Vibration on confirmations, where the device supports it.

  EXPORT            Your transaction history (CSV) or a full copy of your save
                    (JSON).

  RESET ACCOUNT     Back to $10,000 with a brand new market. Wipes positions,
                    orders, history and notes. Asks for confirmation first.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.8  BADGES, STREAKS AND RIVALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Twelve badges mark milestones -- your first trade, five positions, four
sectors, +10% and +25% returns, a filled limit order, your first triggered
alert, a three-day streak. They live on the Portfolio tab.

Three simulated rivals appear on the leaderboard with their own equity curves.
They are there to give the number something to be measured against.


================================================================================
4. A FEW THINGS WORTH TRYING
================================================================================

  * Buy one share of a low-volatility utility (NGRD, AQFR) and one share of a
    high-volatility name (VYRA, PWDR). Watch them for ten minutes. The
    difference in how they behave is the entire concept of risk, visible.

  * Place a limit buy well below the market, then close the app for a day.
    See whether it filled while you were gone.

  * Put a stop-loss under a position and leave it. It is the only order type
    that protects you while you are not watching.

  * Turn on Realistic Fills, then buy and immediately sell the same stock.
    Notice you lost money doing nothing. That gap is what frequent trading
    costs in reality.

  * Check your allocation on the Portfolio tab after a few buys. Concentration
    is easy to create by accident and hard to notice without looking.
